Hello friends, 
 
Most relationships do not end. 
They recede. 
Rarely through conflict. Rarely through a dramatic goodbye. More often through miles, obligations, new 
routines, and lives that gradually unfold in different directions. 
A friend moves away. A sibling becomes busy raising a family. A colleague changes jobs. The 
conversations that once happened daily become monthly, then occasional. Somewhere along the way, we 
begin to wonder: 
Are we losing each other? 
 
Distance creates a peculiar challenge. Relationships are built not only from affection, but from shared 
reality. The lunch breaks, the spontaneous phone calls, the small frustrations, and the ordinary moments 
we rarely think about. When those disappear, connection requires intention in place of proximity. 
 
Yet perhaps growing apart is not always a sign that something has gone wrong. 
The people we love continue becoming themselves. So do we. 
The college friend who knew our youthful ambitions carries a version of us that no longer entirely exists. 
We carry versions of them, too. Time changes us all. The remarkable thing is not that relationships 
transform, but that they remain meaningful despite the transformation. 
 
This week's reflection is simple: 
Not every relationship is meant to remain exactly as it was. 
Some become quieter. 
Some become less frequent. 
Some move from daily presence to cherished memory. 
That does not erase what was real. 
A friendship that carried you through an important chapter of life does not become less valuable because 
the chapter ended. 
Perhaps maturity is learning to hold relationships with open hands. To appreciate what they were, honor 
what they became, and make peace with what they can no longer be. 
 
A question to sit with this week: 
Are there relationships you have been grieving for what they no longer are, when perhaps they are simply 
becoming something different? 
 
After all, every person who truly mattered leaves something behind. 
And every person who truly mattered takes something with them. 
Just enough to remind us that, for a little while, our lives were wonderfully intertwined. 
 
Until next week, 
A Thoughtful Hermit
